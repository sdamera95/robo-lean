# Robo Lean

Robo Lean is a Lean 4 library with pinned Mathlib, starting from the mathematical core of Murray, Li, and Sastry's *A Mathematical Introduction to Robotic Manipulation*. The [roadmap](research/reports/lean-library-roadmap.md), [coverage ledger](research/reports/mls-coverage-ledger.md), and [geometry conventions](research/reports/geometry-conventions.md) define the scope and assumptions.

## Library contents

### Rigid transforms and the rotation exponential

The library provides proper rotations and rigid-transform group/action laws in [RigidTransform](RoboLean/Geometry/RigidTransform.lean), a [homogeneous-matrix equivalence](RoboLean/Geometry/Homogeneous.lean), [cross-product preservation](RoboLean/Geometry/CrossProduct.lean), and [hat/vee maps](RoboLean/Geometry/Hat.lean). [HatPowers](RoboLean/Geometry/HatPowers.lean) proves the Euclidean square/cube identities, [RotationExp](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationExp.lean) packages the actual matrix exponential in `SO(3)` and proves its fixed-axis trajectory equation, and [Rodrigues](RoboLean/Geometry/Rodrigues.lean) gives unit, nonzero, and zero-axis formulas. [Rigid-transform examples](RoboLean/Examples/RigidTransform.lean) and [exponential examples](RoboLean/Examples/RotationExp.lean) state exact convention checks. [Smoke](RoboLean/Smoke.lean) remains the algebra setup check. [IsometryRigidBody](RoboLean/Geometry/IsometryRigidBody.lean) states Definition 2.1 for maps of `ℝ³` and proves its converse: the maps that preserve lengths and cross products are exactly the point actions of `SE(3)`; [IsometryConverse](RoboLean/Examples/IsometryConverse.lean) checks the reflection of p. 21, which preserves lengths and is excluded.

### Rotation representations

[RotationSurjectivity](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationSurjectivity.lean) proves unit-axis exponential representations for every proper rotation, including identity and half-turns, from a fixed unit axis of every proper rotation ([RotationAxis](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationAxis.lean)) and an orthonormal basis adapted to it ([RotationPlane](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationPlane.lean)). [RotationLog](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationLog.lean), [RotationParameters](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationParameters.lean), and [RotationAxisUniqueness](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationAxisUniqueness.lean) state principal-angle formulas, periodicity, and the exact uniqueness limits; [rotation-recovery examples](RoboLean/Examples/RotationLog.lean) check the identity and half-turn endpoints. [ElementaryRotations](RoboLean/Geometry/ElementaryRotations.lean) identifies the displayed axis-rotation matrices with actual exponentials; [EulerAngles](RoboLean/Geometry/EulerAngles.lean) proves forward Z-Y-Z formulas and a generic inverse requiring a positive radial term. [UnitQuaternion](RoboLean/Geometry/UnitQuaternion.lean) covers quaternion multiplication and the norm-one group, and [QuaternionRotation](RoboLean/Geometry/QuaternionRotation.lean) the rotation `v ↦ q v q*` of a unit quaternion: the half-angle quaternion `(cos(θ/2), ω sin(θ/2))` represents `exp(ω̂θ)`, the axis and angle are recovered with their endpoint cases, and the map is a homomorphism onto `SO(3)` that is exactly two-to-one. [CayleyParameters](RoboLean/Geometry/CayleyParameters.lean) gives the Cayley parameterization of Exercise 5 with its printed entries and its inverse where `R + I` is invertible. [PlanarRigidMotion](RoboLean/Geometry/PlanarRigidMotion.lean) and [PlanarRotationCircle](RoboLean/Geometry/PlanarRotationCircle.lean) cover the planar exercises 10–11: `SO(2)` as the unit circle with its exponential, the planar group `SE(2)` with its homogeneous representation and its embedding into `SE(3)`, the exponential of planar twists, poles and the planar adjoint; [PlanarRigidVelocity](RoboLean/Geometry/PlanarRigidVelocity.lean) shows that planar spatial and body velocities are twists. [RotationEigenvalues](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationEigenvalues.lean) and [RotationCharpoly](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationCharpoly.lean) give the characteristic polynomials of `ω̂` and of every rotation (Exercise 3: eigenvalues `0, ±i` and `1, e^{±iθ}`), [RotationProjection](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationProjection.lean) the projection maps and the null space of `I − e^{ω̂θ}` of Exercise 9, [QuaternionRate](RoboLean/Geometry/QuaternionRate.lean) the quaternion rate equation `Q̇Q* = (0, ω/2)` of Exercise 6(e), [RigidBodyDegrees](RoboLean/Geometry/RigidBodyDegrees.lean) the `(n + n²)/2` degrees of freedom of Exercise 7, and [RotationNoGlobalChart](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationNoGlobalChart.lean) the compactness of `SO(3)` and the nonexistence of a global three-parameter chart (p. 33); the ZYX remark of p. 33 at both `θ = ±π/2` is in [ZYXSingularity](RoboLean/Examples/ZYXSingularity.lean), Example 2.1 in [RotationAboutLine](RoboLean/Examples/RotationAboutLine.lean) and Exercise 16 (the pose and velocities of Figure 2.17's manipulator) in [TwistTwoDofManipulator](RoboLean/Examples/TwistTwoDofManipulator.lean) and [TwistTwoDofVelocity](RoboLean/Examples/TwistTwoDofVelocity.lean).

### Twists and exponential coordinates

The twist foundation identifies ordered `(v, ω)` coordinates with an independent `se(3)` matrix carrier in [Twist](RoboLean/Geometry/Twist.lean). [TwistExp](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistExp.lean) uses the actual matrix exponential; [TwistExpClosedForm](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistExpClosedForm.lean) covers unit, nonunit, and zero-angular cases, and [TwistExpJacobian](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistExpJacobian.lean) proves the unit-axis translation coefficient invertible for `0 < θ < 2π`. [TwistSurjectivity](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistSurjectivity.lean) proves every rigid transform is attained, while [TwistLog](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistLog.lean) selects a noncomputable principal exponential coordinate that reconstructs the transform. [TwistTrajectory](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistTrajectory.lean) proves the differential equation and initial value of constant-twist trajectories (MLS (2.25)–(2.26), (2.37), (2.45)), and [twist examples](RoboLean/Examples/Twist.lean) check the coordinate order and the adjoint's linear part on exact inputs.

### Adjoints, brackets, and velocities

[TwistAdjoint](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistAdjoint.lean) and [TwistExpAdjoint](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistExpAdjoint.lean) connect coordinate adjoints, matrix conjugation, and actual exponentials. [TwistBracket](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistBracket.lean) and [AdjointBracket](RoboLean/Geometry/AdjointBracket.lean) connect the coordinate Lie bracket to matrix commutators and adjoint transformation. [RotationVelocity](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationVelocity.lean) (spatial and body angular velocities, Lemma 2.12), [RigidVelocity](RoboLean/Geometry/RigidVelocity.lean) (spatial and body velocities of pose curves, (2.53)–(2.61)) and [RigidVelocityComposition](RoboLean/Geometry/RigidVelocityComposition.lean) (Propositions 2.14–2.15 and Lemma 2.16) derive point, spatial/body, product, and inverse laws from actual derivative hypotheses; Example 2.5 is worked numerically in [Velocity](RoboLean/Examples/Velocity.lean) and symbolically in [PlanarVelocity](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarVelocity.lean). [HybridVelocity](RoboLean/Geometry/HybridVelocity.lean) treats the hybrid velocity `(ṗ, ωˢ)` of Exercise 18: its relation to the body and spatial velocities, its composition rule and its behavior under changes of the spatial and body frames; [HybridVelocityWrench](RoboLean/Geometry/HybridVelocityWrench.lean) gives wrenches in hybrid coordinates with their power and change-of-frame formulas.

### Screws

[TwistScrew](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistScrew.lean), [ScrewAxis](RoboLean/Geometry/ScrewAxis.lean), and [NormalizedTwist](RoboLean/Geometry/NormalizedTwist.lean) make finite pitch, affine axes, full-twist normalization, pure translation, and stationary cases explicit. [TwistScrewMotion](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistScrewMotion.lean) identifies independently defined geometric motion with the actual exponential and proves the fixed-line mechanism formulas. [Chasles](RoboLean/Geometry/Chasles.lean) gives geometric screw-displacement existence; [ScrewConjugation](RoboLean/Geometry/ScrewConjugation.lean) proves frame-change and signed-axis conventions for that motion. [TwistScrewInvariants](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistScrewInvariants.lean) shows how pitch, magnitude and finite axes behave under frame changes and signed scaling, and [screw examples](RoboLean/Examples/Screw.lean) check the parameters on exact inputs.

### Wrenches and reciprocity

[Wrench](RoboLean/Geometry/Wrench.lean), [WrenchScrew](RoboLean/Geometry/WrenchScrew.lean), and [Reciprocal](RoboLean/Geometry/Reciprocal.lean) cover force/torque transformations, power duality, wrench-screw parameters, and reciprocal coordinate spaces; [WrenchFrameInvariants](RoboLean/Geometry/WrenchFrameInvariants.lean) carries the wrench screw coordinates through a frame change. [WrenchWork](RoboLean/Geometry/WrenchWork.lean) proves actual interval-work identities with derivative and integrability hypotheses. [WrenchReciprocityGeometry](RoboLean/Geometry/WrenchReciprocityGeometry.lean) adds geometric screw models and a common-normal formula for a given common normal, and [ScrewCommonNormal](RoboLean/Geometry/ScrewCommonNormal.lean) constructs the common normal of two axes, so that the reciprocal product (2.72) equals the twist–wrench pairing for every pair of nonparallel finite screws (Proposition 2.18), with parallel and infinite-pitch screws treated apart. Exact examples include parameterized planar mechanisms; [wrench transfer](RoboLean/Examples/Wrench.lean), [wrench screw](RoboLean/Examples/WrenchScrew.lean) and [reciprocal](RoboLean/Examples/Reciprocal.lean) examples check the conventions against independently computed values.

### Manipulator kinematics

[SerialChain](RoboLean/Kinematics/SerialChain.lean) defines serial chains and product-of-exponentials forward kinematics (MLS Chapter 3); [Jacobian](RoboLean/Kinematics/Jacobian.lean) derives the spatial and body Jacobians from actual derivatives and [ForcePullback](RoboLean/Kinematics/ForcePullback.lean) the joint torques of an end-effector wrench. [Workspace](RoboLean/Kinematics/Workspace.lean), [PadenKahan](RoboLean/Kinematics/PadenKahan.lean) (Subproblems 1–3), [Singularity](RoboLean/Kinematics/Singularity.lean), [ClosedChain](RoboLean/Kinematics/ClosedChain.lean) and [Manipulability](RoboLean/Kinematics/Manipulability.lean) cover workspaces with explicit quantifiers, inverse-kinematics subproblems with their branch conditions, singular configurations, the closed-chain structure equation and manipulability measures; [MapJacobian](RoboLean/Kinematics/MapJacobian.lean) gives the Jacobian of a coordinate map and its rate, used by workspace control. The planar two-link arm is worked out in [PlanarTwoLink](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLink.lean) (forward kinematics), [PlanarTwoLinkWorkspace](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkWorkspace.lean) (the reachable annulus and a design certificate), [PlanarTwoLinkSingularity](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkSingularity.lean) and [PlanarTwoLinkInverseKinematics](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkInverseKinematics.lean) (eq. (3.16)), and the elbow manipulator of Examples 3.2 and 3.5 in [ElbowManipulator](RoboLean/Examples/ElbowManipulator.lean). [ExpSmooth](RoboLean/Geometry/ExpSmooth.lean) and [SerialChainSmooth](RoboLean/Kinematics/SerialChainSmooth.lean) prove that the matrix exponential along a line, the rotation and twist exponentials and the product-of-exponentials forward kinematics of every serial chain are smooth of every order, analytic included, and [RollingHandSmooth](RoboLean/Grasp/RollingHandSmooth.lean) carries this to the grasp kinematics (5.41), where the contact coordinates read from a finger become a `C¹` function of its joint angles at a contact. [ElbowManipulatorSolutions](RoboLean/Examples/ElbowManipulatorSolutions.lean) proves the eight solutions of Example 3.5: at most eight modulo `2π` under shoulder and wrist regularity, exactly eight when the elbow reaches the target strictly inside its range. [ParallelMechanism](RoboLean/Kinematics/ParallelMechanism.lean) extends the closed chains to `k` chains with the stacked structure equations (3.68), kinematic singularities where the rank of the structure equations drops, and actuator singularities; [Gruebler](RoboLean/Kinematics/Gruebler.lean) states Gruebler's formula (3.66) as a count of independent velocity constraints, [SelfMotion](RoboLean/Kinematics/SelfMotion.lean) the self-motions of (3.62), with Example 3.17 in [PlanarThreeLinkSelfMotion](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarThreeLinkSelfMotion.lean) and the four-bar linkage's uncertainty configuration in [FourBarUncertainty](RoboLean/Examples/FourBarUncertainty.lean). [JacobianDerivative](RoboLean/Kinematics/JacobianDerivative.lean) proves the derivative form of the Jacobians, eqs. (3.51)–(3.55): the Fréchet derivative of the forward kinematics in a joint direction `v` is `(Jˢ(θ)v)^ g_st(θ) = g_st(θ)(Jᵇ(θ)v)^`, and the partial derivatives satisfy `∂g_st/∂θᵢ g_st⁻¹ = (ξᵢ')^` and `g_st⁻¹ ∂g_st/∂θᵢ = (ξᵢ†)^`, with the planar two-link arm in [PlanarTwoLinkJacobianDerivative](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkJacobianDerivative.lean). [DenavitHartenberg](RoboLean/Kinematics/DenavitHartenberg.lean) proves that a chain of Denavit–Hartenberg transforms (3.6) is a product of exponentials with the twists (3.10); [ReferenceConfiguration](RoboLean/Kinematics/ReferenceConfiguration.lean) changes base frames, tool frames and the reference configuration (p. 91, Exercise 8); [ResolvedRate](RoboLean/Kinematics/ResolvedRate.lean) proves that integrating the resolved-rate equation (3.57) moves the end-effector along the workspace path, under hypotheses the book omits; [RedundantManipulator](RoboLean/Kinematics/RedundantManipulator.lean) gives the minimum-norm rates `J†Vˢ` and the statics (3.63) of redundant manipulators; [PadenKahanVariants](RoboLean/Kinematics/PadenKahanVariants.lean), [SingularityConditions](RoboLean/Kinematics/SingularityConditions.lean), [ManipulabilityFrames](RoboLean/Kinematics/ManipulabilityFrames.lean) and [EulerSingularity](RoboLean/Kinematics/EulerSingularity.lean) cover Exercises 5–7 and 14–19; [StewartPlatform](RoboLean/Kinematics/StewartPlatform.lean) models the spherical joint and one leg of the Stewart platform's structure equation (3.72), with its inverse kinematics, the leg Jacobian (3.73) and the coplanar actuator singularity. The SCARA of Examples 3.1, 3.3, 3.6 and 3.10 is in [ScaraKinematics](RoboLean/Examples/ScaraKinematics.lean), the Stanford arm's Jacobian (3.58) in [StanfordArm](RoboLean/Examples/StanfordArm.lean), the workspaces of Example 3.4 in [PlanarKinematicsWorkspace](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarKinematicsWorkspace.lean), isotropic points in [PlanarKinematicsIsotropic](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarKinematicsIsotropic.lean) and the Gruebler counts of Exercises 20–22 in [ParallelGruebler](RoboLean/Examples/ParallelGruebler.lean). [SphericalWristAngles](RoboLean/Kinematics/SphericalWristAngles.lean) proves the ZYZ Euler angles at the endpoints `β = 0, π` and that three-axis angles reach every rotation, and [SphericalWrist](RoboLean/Kinematics/SphericalWrist.lean) that a manipulator with a spherical wrist has `W_D = W_R` and `W = W_R × SO(3)` (p. 96), with the elbow manipulator in [SphericalWristElbow](RoboLean/Examples/SphericalWristElbow.lean); [ElbowExercisesSpheres](RoboLean/Kinematics/ElbowExercisesSpheres.lean) counts the image points of Subproblem 4 and [ElbowExerciseIsotropy](RoboLean/Examples/ElbowExerciseIsotropy.lean) finds the isotropic points of the elbow manipulator without a wrist (Exercise 13(b)–(c)). [ParallelExerciseSliderCrank](RoboLean/Examples/ParallelExerciseSliderCrank.lean) works Exercise 20 for the slider–crank (structure equations, twists, the dead centres as kinematic singularities and the actuator singularities `cos(θ₁ + θ₂) = 0`, which exist exactly when `l₂ ≤ l₁`), [ParallelExerciseTruss](RoboLean/Examples/ParallelExerciseTruss.lean) Exercise 21 for the variable geometry truss (structure equations, leg lengths, twists and singularities), and [ParallelExercisesUJoint](RoboLean/Kinematics/ParallelExercisesUJoint.lean) Exercise 22(d) for the Stewart platform with U-joints; [FigureManipulatorThreeDof](RoboLean/Examples/FigureManipulatorThreeDof.lean) and [FigureManipulatorInclined](RoboLean/Examples/FigureManipulatorInclined.lean) give the forward kinematics and Jacobians of the four manipulators of Figure 3.23, with the inverse kinematics of three of them. [InverseElbow](RoboLean/Examples/InverseElbow.lean), [Rhino](RoboLean/Examples/Rhino.lean), [InverseElbowSingular](RoboLean/Examples/InverseElbowSingular.lean) and [RhinoSingular](RoboLean/Examples/RhinoSingular.lean) work Exercise 4 for the inverse elbow and the Rhino robot of Figure 3.24 (forward kinematics, spatial Jacobians, inverse-kinematics steps by the Paden–Kahan subproblems, both singular with the elbow stretched or folded, and the Rhino also when its slide lies in the plane of the arm), [ExerciseThreeStanford](RoboLean/Examples/ExerciseThreeStanford.lean) and [ExerciseThreeElbow](RoboLean/Examples/ExerciseThreeElbow.lean) complete the Stanford arm's inverse kinematics through Subproblem 5 and the elbow manipulator's spatial Jacobian, [OffsetElbow](RoboLean/Examples/OffsetElbow.lean) solves the elbow with non-intersecting shoulder axes (Exercise 5), [FigureManipulatorInclinedInverse](RoboLean/Examples/FigureManipulatorInclinedInverse.lean) gives the body Jacobian and inverse kinematics of Figure 3.23(ii), [ExerciseThreeScara](RoboLean/Examples/ExerciseThreeScara.lean) the SCARA's extension (Exercise 7), [ChapterThreeExercises](RoboLean/Kinematics/ChapterThreeExercises.lean) the task-oriented manipulability of Exercise 12 through the largest singular value, and [TrussSingular](RoboLean/Examples/TrussSingular.lean) the truss's kinematic singularity at one leg of zero length.

### Open-chain dynamics

[RigidBodyInertia](RoboLean/Dynamics/RigidBodyInertia.lean), [InertiaTensor](RoboLean/Dynamics/InertiaTensor.lean), [MassMatrix](RoboLean/Dynamics/MassMatrix.lean) and [MassMatrixDerivative](RoboLean/Dynamics/MassMatrixDerivative.lean) build the manipulator inertia matrix of MLS Chapter 4 from link inertias and link Jacobians, with its differentiability and frame freedom. [Christoffel](RoboLean/Dynamics/Christoffel.lean) proves the skew symmetry of `Ṁ − 2C` for the Christoffel Coriolis matrix, [Lagrangian](RoboLean/Dynamics/Lagrangian.lean) gives Lagrange's equations and the equations of motion, [NewtonEuler](RoboLean/Dynamics/NewtonEuler.lean) the rigid-body Newton–Euler equations, derived from the inertial-frame Newton and Euler laws with Euler's equation in spatial coordinates in [NewtonEulerSpatial](RoboLean/Dynamics/NewtonEulerSpatial.lean) and the spatial-momentum form of Exercise 5 in [NewtonEulerSpatialMomentum](RoboLean/Dynamics/NewtonEulerSpatialMomentum.lean), and [PotentialEnergy](RoboLean/Dynamics/PotentialEnergy.lean) the gravity term; [InertiaFrameChange](RoboLean/Dynamics/InertiaFrameChange.lean) gives the generalized inertia in a second body frame (Exercise 3), [ViscousFriction](RoboLean/Dynamics/ViscousFriction.lean) viscous joint friction and end-effector forces with the energy balance, and [PendulumExercises](RoboLean/Dynamics/PendulumExercises.lean) the pendulum on a wire and the double pendulum of Exercise 1. [KineticEnergyIntegral](RoboLean/Dynamics/KineticEnergyIntegral.lean) derives the kinetic energy (4.8)–(4.9) from the volume integral of a mass distribution, with the generalized inertia of a body frame anywhere, [InertiaIntegral](RoboLean/Dynamics/InertiaIntegral.lean) computes the inertia tensors of the homogeneous ellipsoid and cylinder of Exercise 2, and [MassDistributionFrameChange](RoboLean/Dynamics/MassDistributionFrameChange.lean) derives Exercise 3 and the parallel-axis theorem from the mass integrals. For the planar two-link arm of Figure 4.4 ([PlanarTwoLinkDynamics](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkDynamics.lean), [PlanarTwoLinkLagrangian](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkLagrangian.lean)), the three-link arm of Example 4.3 ([ThreeLinkDynamics](RoboLean/Examples/ThreeLinkDynamics.lean), [ThreeLinkGravity](RoboLean/Examples/ThreeLinkGravity.lean)) and the SCARA of Example 4.4 ([ScaraDynamics](RoboLean/Examples/ScaraDynamics.lean)), the matrices computed from the general construction are compared with the printed ones; [SphericalPendulum](RoboLean/Examples/SphericalPendulum.lean) derives Lagrange's equations of Example 4.1.

### Stability and control

[Stability](RoboLean/Control/Stability.lean) and [StabilityTheorems](RoboLean/Control/StabilityTheorems.lean) state the Lyapunov notions of MLS Chapter 4 §4 and Theorems 4.4 and 4.5 (sufficiency), with the comparison bounds of quadratic forms in [QuadraticBounds](RoboLean/Control/QuadraticBounds.lean); [SolutionFlow](RoboLean/Control/SolutionFlow.lean), [LyapunovFlow](RoboLean/Control/LyapunovFlow.lean) and [GlobalFlow](RoboLean/Control/GlobalFlow.lean) supply solution flows, [LaSalle](RoboLean/Control/LaSalle.lean) proves Lasalle's principle (Theorem 4.7), and [LaSalleLocal](RoboLean/Control/LaSalleLocal.lean) its local form on a ball. [ConverseLyapunov](RoboLean/Control/ConverseLyapunov.lean) proves the converse of Theorem 4.5 with three bounds for autonomous Lipschitz fields, and [ConverseLyapunovGeneral](RoboLean/Control/ConverseLyapunovGeneral.lean) the converse with all four bounds for time-varying fields, under the hypothesis of [FlowDerivative](RoboLean/Control/FlowDerivative.lean) that the solution flow is differentiable in the initial state, which is discharged for linear time-varying systems. [GlobalFlowTimeVarying](RoboLean/Control/GlobalFlowTimeVarying.lean) gives global solutions of time-varying Lipschitz fields, [FlowDifferentiable](RoboLean/Control/FlowDifferentiable.lean), [FlowDifferentiableC1](RoboLean/Control/FlowDifferentiableC1.lean) and [FlowJointC1](RoboLean/Control/FlowJointC1.lean) prove differentiable dependence on initial conditions, with a derivative continuous in the time and the initial state, for fields with a continuous bounded derivative, which discharges that hypothesis, [FlowCk](RoboLean/Control/FlowCk.lean) proves that the flow is of class `C^k` in the initial state for a field of class `C^k` with bounded derivatives on a finite-dimensional space, with the second variational equation in [FlowSecondDerivative](RoboLean/Control/FlowSecondDerivative.lean), and [ConverseLyapunovC1](RoboLean/Control/ConverseLyapunovC1.lean) states the four-bound converse for such fields without it, with the damped pendulum ([FlowDifferentiablePendulum](RoboLean/Examples/FlowDifferentiablePendulum.lean)) and `ẋᵢ = −2xᵢ + sin xᵢ` ([ConverseLyapunovC1Instance](RoboLean/Examples/ConverseLyapunovC1Instance.lean)) as instances. [Linearization](RoboLean/Control/Linearization.lean) and [LinearizationTimeVarying](RoboLean/Control/LinearizationTimeVarying.lean), with [StateTransition](RoboLean/Control/StateTransition.lean), [LinearTimeVarying](RoboLean/Control/LinearTimeVarying.lean) and the eigenvalue decay bound of [MatrixExponentialSpectrum](RoboLean/Control/MatrixExponentialSpectrum.lean), prove Theorem 4.6 for constant and time-varying linearizations; [StateTransitionPiecewise](RoboLean/Control/StateTransitionPiecewise.lean) shows that, among piecewise continuous `A(t)`, the library's state-transition matrices exist only for continuous `A`. The examples are the damped harmonic oscillator of Example 4.5 ([HarmonicOscillator](RoboLean/Examples/HarmonicOscillator.lean)), the nonlinear spring of Examples 4.6–4.7 ([NonlinearSpring](RoboLean/Examples/NonlinearSpring.lean)), the damped pendulum by linearization ([Linearization](RoboLean/Examples/Linearization.lean)), the time-varying system `ż = −(1 + sin t) z` ([LinearizationTimeVarying](RoboLean/Examples/LinearizationTimeVarying.lean)) and, in [ConverseLyapunovGeneral](RoboLean/Examples/ConverseLyapunovGeneral.lean), the four-bound converse on that system and a piecewise continuous rate without a state-transition matrix.

The manipulator of §5 is a first-order system in [ManipulatorSystem](RoboLean/Control/ManipulatorSystem.lean), with the error dynamics of computed torque in [ErrorDynamics](RoboLean/Control/ErrorDynamics.lean) and shared matrix lemmas in [MatrixAnalysis](RoboLean/Control/MatrixAnalysis.lean). Its controllers are in [ComputedTorque](RoboLean/Control/ComputedTorque.lean) (Proposition 4.8), [PDControl](RoboLean/Control/PDControl.lean), [PDAsymptotic](RoboLean/Control/PDAsymptotic.lean) and [PDFlow](RoboLean/Control/PDFlow.lean) (Proposition 4.9, with compensation of the gravity term, and the flow of its closed loop under local Lipschitz continuity), [AugmentedPD](RoboLean/Control/AugmentedPD.lean) (Proposition 4.10) and [WorkspaceControl](RoboLean/Control/WorkspaceControl.lean) (workspace dynamics, Lemma 4.11 and workspace computed torque). For every robot `(M, C, N)` of Chapter 6 §5, [RobotSystem](RoboLean/Control/RobotSystem.lean) proves Proposition 4.8, [RobotPDControl](RoboLean/Control/RobotPDControl.lean) Proposition 4.9 and [RobotAugmentedPD](RoboLean/Control/RobotAugmentedPD.lean) Proposition 4.10 (semiglobal, and global when the inertia matrix is constant and the Coriolis matrix is a bounded skew matrix, the class that a uniform bound on the Coriolis matrix forces) with the tracking workspace PD law of Exercise 9; [WorkspacePDControl](RoboLean/Control/WorkspacePDControl.lean) gives the workspace PD law. The examples are a one-link arm ([OneLinkControl](RoboLean/Examples/OneLinkControl.lean)), the planar two-link arm under PD and computed torque ([PlanarTwoLinkControl](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkControl.lean)), in workspace coordinates ([WorkspaceControl](RoboLean/Examples/WorkspaceControl.lean)), in absolute link angles ([RobotPDControl](RoboLean/Examples/RobotPDControl.lean)) and as a robot under augmented PD ([RobotAugmentedPD](RoboLean/Examples/RobotAugmentedPD.lean)); [ThreeLinkGravityPD](RoboLean/Examples/ThreeLinkGravityPD.lean) shows that the bare PD law (4.51) does not hold the three-link arm at rest in gravity, and [AugmentedPDEnergy](RoboLean/Examples/AugmentedPDEnergy.lean) that no single `ε` makes the skewed energy of Proposition 4.10 a global Lyapunov function for the planar arm. [ExponentialArrival](RoboLean/Control/ExponentialArrival.lean) names the constants of Definition 4.3's bound together with the radius of initial errors it covers, derives the time after which a solution stays within a tolerance, and proves that PD control with compensation of the gravity term converges exponentially to every set point on bounded sets of initial errors, with constants independent of the set point (Proposition 4.10 at a constant set point); [PlanarTwoLinkRegulation](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkRegulation.lean) discharges it for the planar two-link arm.

### Design certificates

### Demo

[Supervisor](RoboLean/Demo/Supervisor.lean) is an executable timeout supervisor for the planar two-link arm over rational targets, with core imports only and a line protocol in [Main](RoboLean/Demo/Main.lean); [SupervisorRefines](RoboLean/Demo/SupervisorRefines.lean) proves that its guard decides the workspace guard exactly and that every state it reaches is a reachable state of the design supervisor, so the design invariants and the reachability of commanded targets hold on it, and [PlanarArmFrames](RoboLean/Demo/PlanarArmFrames.lean) joins the kinematic and dynamic frames of the arm. [ArrivalTime](RoboLean/Demo/ArrivalTime.lean) bounds, for the ideal continuous arm held at each commanded set point, the end-effector error by an exponential in time and derives an arrival time and an age bound that certifies arrival, and [ArrivalConstants](RoboLean/Demo/ArrivalConstants.lean) computes explicit constants for the demo's parameters and the age bound they certify; the [demo report](research/reports/planar-arm-demo.md) compares that bound with the demo's own timeout. A Python loop under [demo](demo) simulates the arm under PD control and asks the Lean executable for every supervisory decision: after `lake build`, which also compiles the supervisor to the native executable `planar-arm-supervisor` (run it with `lake exe planar-arm-supervisor`), run `uv run python planar_arm_demo.py` in `demo/`; the loop drives the native executable through `lake exe planar-arm-supervisor` unless the environment variable `ROBO_LEAN_SUPERVISOR` names another command, such as the interpreter `lake env lean --run RoboLean/Demo/Main.lean`. `uv run python veil_differential_test.py` in `demo/` replays random and scripted action traces through both Lean commands and through Veil's executable semantics of the supervisor model (in a separate Veil project, since the two use different Lean versions) and compares the states after every action; it is a test, not a proof. The [demo report](research/reports/planar-arm-demo.md) states what is kernel-checked and what is not; the compiler, the protocol, the floating-point simulation and the harness are unverified.

## Building

Install [elan](https://lean-lang.org/install/), then run from this directory:

```sh
lake exe cache get
lake build
```

`lake build` builds the library and the demo's native executable `planar-arm-supervisor` under `.lake/build/bin/`. Do not run `lake update`: the Mathlib revision is pinned in `lakefile.toml` and the resolved dependencies are checked in as `lake-manifest.json`. A pin change is a recorded decision, not a routine step.
