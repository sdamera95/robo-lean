# Robo Lean

Robo Lean is a Lean 4 library with pinned Mathlib, starting from the mathematical core of Murray, Li, and Sastry's *A Mathematical Introduction to Robotic Manipulation*. The [roadmap](research/reports/lean-library-roadmap.md), [coverage ledger](research/reports/mls-coverage-ledger.md), and [geometry conventions](research/reports/geometry-conventions.md) define the scope and assumptions.

## Library contents

### Rigid transforms and the rotation exponential

The library provides proper rotations and rigid-transform group/action laws in [RigidTransform](RoboLean/Geometry/RigidTransform.lean), a [homogeneous-matrix equivalence](RoboLean/Geometry/Homogeneous.lean), [cross-product preservation](RoboLean/Geometry/CrossProduct.lean), and [hat/vee maps](RoboLean/Geometry/Hat.lean). [HatPowers](RoboLean/Geometry/HatPowers.lean) proves the Euclidean square/cube identities, [RotationExp](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationExp.lean) packages the actual matrix exponential in `SO(3)` and proves its fixed-axis trajectory equation, and [Rodrigues](RoboLean/Geometry/Rodrigues.lean) gives unit, nonzero, and zero-axis formulas. [Rigid-transform examples](RoboLean/Examples/RigidTransform.lean) and [exponential examples](RoboLean/Examples/RotationExp.lean) state exact convention checks. [Smoke](RoboLean/Smoke.lean) remains the algebra setup check. [IsometryRigidBody](RoboLean/Geometry/IsometryRigidBody.lean) states Definition 2.1 for maps of `ℝ³` and proves its converse: the maps that preserve lengths and cross products are exactly the point actions of `SE(3)`; [IsometryConverse](RoboLean/Examples/IsometryConverse.lean) checks the reflection of p. 21, which preserves lengths and is excluded.

### Rotation representations

[RotationSurjectivity](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationSurjectivity.lean) proves unit-axis exponential representations for every proper rotation, including identity and half-turns, from a fixed unit axis of every proper rotation ([RotationAxis](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationAxis.lean)) and an orthonormal basis adapted to it ([RotationPlane](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationPlane.lean)). [RotationLog](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationLog.lean), [RotationParameters](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationParameters.lean), and [RotationAxisUniqueness](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationAxisUniqueness.lean) state principal-angle formulas, periodicity, and the exact uniqueness limits; [rotation-recovery examples](RoboLean/Examples/RotationLog.lean) check the identity and half-turn endpoints. [ElementaryRotations](RoboLean/Geometry/ElementaryRotations.lean) identifies the displayed axis-rotation matrices with actual exponentials; [EulerAngles](RoboLean/Geometry/EulerAngles.lean) proves forward Z-Y-Z formulas and a generic inverse requiring a positive radial term. [UnitQuaternion](RoboLean/Geometry/UnitQuaternion.lean) covers quaternion multiplication and the norm-one group, and [QuaternionRotation](RoboLean/Geometry/QuaternionRotation.lean) the rotation `v ↦ q v q*` of a unit quaternion: the half-angle quaternion `(cos(θ/2), ω sin(θ/2))` represents `exp(ω̂θ)`, the axis and angle are recovered with their endpoint cases, and the map is a homomorphism onto `SO(3)` that is exactly two-to-one. [CayleyParameters](RoboLean/Geometry/CayleyParameters.lean) gives the Cayley parameterization of Exercise 5 with its printed entries and its inverse where `R + I` is invertible. [PlanarRigidMotion](RoboLean/Geometry/PlanarRigidMotion.lean) and [PlanarRotationCircle](RoboLean/Geometry/PlanarRotationCircle.lean) cover the planar exercises 10–11: `SO(2)` as the unit circle with its exponential, the planar group `SE(2)` with its homogeneous representation and its embedding into `SE(3)`, the exponential of planar twists, poles and the planar adjoint; [PlanarRigidVelocity](RoboLean/Geometry/PlanarRigidVelocity.lean) shows that planar spatial and body velocities are twists.

### Twists and exponential coordinates

The twist foundation identifies ordered `(v, ω)` coordinates with an independent `se(3)` matrix carrier in [Twist](RoboLean/Geometry/Twist.lean). [TwistExp](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistExp.lean) uses the actual matrix exponential; [TwistExpClosedForm](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistExpClosedForm.lean) covers unit, nonunit, and zero-angular cases, and [TwistExpJacobian](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistExpJacobian.lean) proves the unit-axis translation coefficient invertible for `0 < θ < 2π`. [TwistSurjectivity](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistSurjectivity.lean) proves every rigid transform is attained, while [TwistLog](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistLog.lean) selects a noncomputable principal exponential coordinate that reconstructs the transform. [TwistTrajectory](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistTrajectory.lean) proves the differential equation and initial value of constant-twist trajectories (MLS (2.25)–(2.26), (2.37), (2.45)), and [twist examples](RoboLean/Examples/Twist.lean) check the coordinate order and the adjoint's linear part on exact inputs.

### Adjoints, brackets, and velocities

[TwistAdjoint](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistAdjoint.lean) and [TwistExpAdjoint](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistExpAdjoint.lean) connect coordinate adjoints, matrix conjugation, and actual exponentials. [TwistBracket](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistBracket.lean) and [AdjointBracket](RoboLean/Geometry/AdjointBracket.lean) connect the coordinate Lie bracket to matrix commutators and adjoint transformation. [RotationVelocity](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationVelocity.lean) (spatial and body angular velocities, Lemma 2.12), [RigidVelocity](RoboLean/Geometry/RigidVelocity.lean) (spatial and body velocities of pose curves, (2.53)–(2.61)) and [RigidVelocityComposition](RoboLean/Geometry/RigidVelocityComposition.lean) (Propositions 2.14–2.15 and Lemma 2.16) derive point, spatial/body, product, and inverse laws from actual derivative hypotheses; Example 2.5 is worked numerically in [Velocity](RoboLean/Examples/Velocity.lean) and symbolically in [PlanarVelocity](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarVelocity.lean). [HybridVelocity](RoboLean/Geometry/HybridVelocity.lean) treats the hybrid velocity `(ṗ, ωˢ)` of Exercise 18: its relation to the body and spatial velocities, its composition rule and its behavior under changes of the spatial and body frames; [HybridVelocityWrench](RoboLean/Geometry/HybridVelocityWrench.lean) gives wrenches in hybrid coordinates with their power and change-of-frame formulas.

### Screws

[TwistScrew](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistScrew.lean), [ScrewAxis](RoboLean/Geometry/ScrewAxis.lean), and [NormalizedTwist](RoboLean/Geometry/NormalizedTwist.lean) make finite pitch, affine axes, full-twist normalization, pure translation, and stationary cases explicit. [TwistScrewMotion](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistScrewMotion.lean) identifies independently defined geometric motion with the actual exponential and proves the fixed-line mechanism formulas. [Chasles](RoboLean/Geometry/Chasles.lean) gives geometric screw-displacement existence; [ScrewConjugation](RoboLean/Geometry/ScrewConjugation.lean) proves frame-change and signed-axis conventions for that motion. [TwistScrewInvariants](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistScrewInvariants.lean) shows how pitch, magnitude and finite axes behave under frame changes and signed scaling, and [screw examples](RoboLean/Examples/Screw.lean) check the parameters on exact inputs.

### Wrenches and reciprocity

[Wrench](RoboLean/Geometry/Wrench.lean), [WrenchScrew](RoboLean/Geometry/WrenchScrew.lean), and [Reciprocal](RoboLean/Geometry/Reciprocal.lean) cover force/torque transformations, power duality, wrench-screw parameters, and reciprocal coordinate spaces; [WrenchFrameInvariants](RoboLean/Geometry/WrenchFrameInvariants.lean) carries the wrench screw coordinates through a frame change. [WrenchWork](RoboLean/Geometry/WrenchWork.lean) proves actual interval-work identities with derivative and integrability hypotheses. [WrenchReciprocityGeometry](RoboLean/Geometry/WrenchReciprocityGeometry.lean) adds geometric screw models and a common-normal formula for a given common normal, and [ScrewCommonNormal](RoboLean/Geometry/ScrewCommonNormal.lean) constructs the common normal of two axes, so that the reciprocal product (2.72) equals the twist–wrench pairing for every pair of nonparallel finite screws (Proposition 2.18), with parallel and infinite-pitch screws treated apart. Exact examples include parameterized planar mechanisms; [wrench transfer](RoboLean/Examples/Wrench.lean), [wrench screw](RoboLean/Examples/WrenchScrew.lean) and [reciprocal](RoboLean/Examples/Reciprocal.lean) examples check the conventions against independently computed values.

### Manipulator kinematics

[SerialChain](RoboLean/Kinematics/SerialChain.lean) defines serial chains and product-of-exponentials forward kinematics (MLS Chapter 3); [Jacobian](RoboLean/Kinematics/Jacobian.lean) derives the spatial and body Jacobians from actual derivatives and [ForcePullback](RoboLean/Kinematics/ForcePullback.lean) the joint torques of an end-effector wrench. [Workspace](RoboLean/Kinematics/Workspace.lean), [PadenKahan](RoboLean/Kinematics/PadenKahan.lean) (Subproblems 1–3), [Singularity](RoboLean/Kinematics/Singularity.lean), [ClosedChain](RoboLean/Kinematics/ClosedChain.lean) and [Manipulability](RoboLean/Kinematics/Manipulability.lean) cover workspaces with explicit quantifiers, inverse-kinematics subproblems with their branch conditions, singular configurations, the closed-chain structure equation and manipulability measures; [MapJacobian](RoboLean/Kinematics/MapJacobian.lean) gives the Jacobian of a coordinate map and its rate, used by workspace control. The planar two-link arm is worked out in [PlanarTwoLink](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLink.lean) (forward kinematics), [PlanarTwoLinkWorkspace](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkWorkspace.lean) (the reachable annulus and a design certificate), [PlanarTwoLinkSingularity](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkSingularity.lean) and [PlanarTwoLinkInverseKinematics](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkInverseKinematics.lean) (eq. (3.16)), and the elbow manipulator of Examples 3.2 and 3.5 in [ElbowManipulator](RoboLean/Examples/ElbowManipulator.lean). [ExpSmooth](RoboLean/Geometry/ExpSmooth.lean) and [SerialChainSmooth](RoboLean/Kinematics/SerialChainSmooth.lean) prove that the matrix exponential along a line, the rotation and twist exponentials and the product-of-exponentials forward kinematics of every serial chain are smooth of every order, analytic included, and [RollingHandSmooth](RoboLean/Grasp/RollingHandSmooth.lean) carries this to the grasp kinematics (5.41), where the contact coordinates read from a finger become a `C¹` function of its joint angles at a contact. [ElbowManipulatorSolutions](RoboLean/Examples/ElbowManipulatorSolutions.lean) proves the eight solutions of Example 3.5: at most eight modulo `2π` under shoulder and wrist regularity, exactly eight when the elbow reaches the target strictly inside its range. [ParallelMechanism](RoboLean/Kinematics/ParallelMechanism.lean) extends the closed chains to `k` chains with the stacked structure equations (3.68), kinematic singularities where the rank of the structure equations drops, and actuator singularities; [Gruebler](RoboLean/Kinematics/Gruebler.lean) states Gruebler's formula (3.66) as a count of independent velocity constraints, [SelfMotion](RoboLean/Kinematics/SelfMotion.lean) the self-motions of (3.62), with Example 3.17 in [PlanarThreeLinkSelfMotion](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarThreeLinkSelfMotion.lean) and the four-bar linkage's uncertainty configuration in [FourBarUncertainty](RoboLean/Examples/FourBarUncertainty.lean). [JacobianDerivative](RoboLean/Kinematics/JacobianDerivative.lean) proves the derivative form of the Jacobians, eqs. (3.51)–(3.55): the Fréchet derivative of the forward kinematics in a joint direction `v` is `(Jˢ(θ)v)^ g_st(θ) = g_st(θ)(Jᵇ(θ)v)^`, and the partial derivatives satisfy `∂g_st/∂θᵢ g_st⁻¹ = (ξᵢ')^` and `g_st⁻¹ ∂g_st/∂θᵢ = (ξᵢ†)^`, with the planar two-link arm in [PlanarTwoLinkJacobianDerivative](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkJacobianDerivative.lean). [DenavitHartenberg](RoboLean/Kinematics/DenavitHartenberg.lean) proves that a chain of Denavit–Hartenberg transforms (3.6) is a product of exponentials with the twists (3.10); [ReferenceConfiguration](RoboLean/Kinematics/ReferenceConfiguration.lean) changes base frames, tool frames and the reference configuration (p. 91, Exercise 8); [ResolvedRate](RoboLean/Kinematics/ResolvedRate.lean) proves that integrating the resolved-rate equation (3.57) moves the end-effector along the workspace path, under hypotheses the book omits; [RedundantManipulator](RoboLean/Kinematics/RedundantManipulator.lean) gives the minimum-norm rates `J†Vˢ` and the statics (3.63) of redundant manipulators; [PadenKahanVariants](RoboLean/Kinematics/PadenKahanVariants.lean), [SingularityConditions](RoboLean/Kinematics/SingularityConditions.lean), [ManipulabilityFrames](RoboLean/Kinematics/ManipulabilityFrames.lean) and [EulerSingularity](RoboLean/Kinematics/EulerSingularity.lean) cover Exercises 5–7 and 14–19; [StewartPlatform](RoboLean/Kinematics/StewartPlatform.lean) models the spherical joint and one leg of the Stewart platform's structure equation (3.72), with its inverse kinematics, the leg Jacobian (3.73) and the coplanar actuator singularity. The SCARA of Examples 3.1, 3.3, 3.6 and 3.10 is in [ScaraKinematics](RoboLean/Examples/ScaraKinematics.lean), the Stanford arm's Jacobian (3.58) in [StanfordArm](RoboLean/Examples/StanfordArm.lean), the workspaces of Example 3.4 in [PlanarKinematicsWorkspace](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarKinematicsWorkspace.lean), isotropic points in [PlanarKinematicsIsotropic](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarKinematicsIsotropic.lean) and the Gruebler counts of Exercises 20–22 in [ParallelGruebler](RoboLean/Examples/ParallelGruebler.lean).

### Open-chain dynamics

[RigidBodyInertia](RoboLean/Dynamics/RigidBodyInertia.lean), [InertiaTensor](RoboLean/Dynamics/InertiaTensor.lean), [MassMatrix](RoboLean/Dynamics/MassMatrix.lean) and [MassMatrixDerivative](RoboLean/Dynamics/MassMatrixDerivative.lean) build the manipulator inertia matrix of MLS Chapter 4 from link inertias and link Jacobians, with its differentiability and frame freedom. [Christoffel](RoboLean/Dynamics/Christoffel.lean) proves the skew symmetry of `Ṁ − 2C` for the Christoffel Coriolis matrix, [Lagrangian](RoboLean/Dynamics/Lagrangian.lean) gives Lagrange's equations and the equations of motion, [NewtonEuler](RoboLean/Dynamics/NewtonEuler.lean) the rigid-body Newton–Euler equations and [PotentialEnergy](RoboLean/Dynamics/PotentialEnergy.lean) the gravity term. For the planar two-link arm of Figure 4.4 ([PlanarTwoLinkDynamics](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkDynamics.lean), [PlanarTwoLinkLagrangian](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkLagrangian.lean)), the three-link arm of Example 4.3 ([ThreeLinkDynamics](RoboLean/Examples/ThreeLinkDynamics.lean), [ThreeLinkGravity](RoboLean/Examples/ThreeLinkGravity.lean)) and the SCARA of Example 4.4 ([ScaraDynamics](RoboLean/Examples/ScaraDynamics.lean)), the matrices computed from the general construction are compared with the printed ones; [SphericalPendulum](RoboLean/Examples/SphericalPendulum.lean) derives Lagrange's equations of Example 4.1.

### Stability and control

[Stability](RoboLean/Control/Stability.lean) and [StabilityTheorems](RoboLean/Control/StabilityTheorems.lean) state the Lyapunov notions of MLS Chapter 4 §4 and Theorems 4.4 and 4.5 (sufficiency), with the comparison bounds of quadratic forms in [QuadraticBounds](RoboLean/Control/QuadraticBounds.lean); [SolutionFlow](RoboLean/Control/SolutionFlow.lean), [LyapunovFlow](RoboLean/Control/LyapunovFlow.lean) and [GlobalFlow](RoboLean/Control/GlobalFlow.lean) supply solution flows, [LaSalle](RoboLean/Control/LaSalle.lean) proves Lasalle's principle (Theorem 4.7), and [LaSalleLocal](RoboLean/Control/LaSalleLocal.lean) its local form on a ball. [ConverseLyapunov](RoboLean/Control/ConverseLyapunov.lean) proves the converse of Theorem 4.5 with three bounds for autonomous Lipschitz fields, and [ConverseLyapunovGeneral](RoboLean/Control/ConverseLyapunovGeneral.lean) the converse with all four bounds for time-varying fields, under the hypothesis of [FlowDerivative](RoboLean/Control/FlowDerivative.lean) that the solution flow is differentiable in the initial state, which is discharged for linear time-varying systems. [GlobalFlowTimeVarying](RoboLean/Control/GlobalFlowTimeVarying.lean) gives global solutions of time-varying Lipschitz fields, [FlowDifferentiable](RoboLean/Control/FlowDifferentiable.lean), [FlowDifferentiableC1](RoboLean/Control/FlowDifferentiableC1.lean) and [FlowJointC1](RoboLean/Control/FlowJointC1.lean) prove differentiable dependence on initial conditions, with a derivative continuous in the time and the initial state, for fields with a continuous bounded derivative, which discharges that hypothesis, and [ConverseLyapunovC1](RoboLean/Control/ConverseLyapunovC1.lean) states the four-bound converse for such fields without it, with the damped pendulum ([FlowDifferentiablePendulum](RoboLean/Examples/FlowDifferentiablePendulum.lean)) and `ẋᵢ = −2xᵢ + sin xᵢ` ([ConverseLyapunovC1Instance](RoboLean/Examples/ConverseLyapunovC1Instance.lean)) as instances. [Linearization](RoboLean/Control/Linearization.lean) and [LinearizationTimeVarying](RoboLean/Control/LinearizationTimeVarying.lean), with [StateTransition](RoboLean/Control/StateTransition.lean), [LinearTimeVarying](RoboLean/Control/LinearTimeVarying.lean) and the eigenvalue decay bound of [MatrixExponentialSpectrum](RoboLean/Control/MatrixExponentialSpectrum.lean), prove Theorem 4.6 for constant and time-varying linearizations; [StateTransitionPiecewise](RoboLean/Control/StateTransitionPiecewise.lean) shows that, among piecewise continuous `A(t)`, the library's state-transition matrices exist only for continuous `A`. The examples are the damped harmonic oscillator of Example 4.5 ([HarmonicOscillator](RoboLean/Examples/HarmonicOscillator.lean)), the nonlinear spring of Examples 4.6–4.7 ([NonlinearSpring](RoboLean/Examples/NonlinearSpring.lean)), the damped pendulum by linearization ([Linearization](RoboLean/Examples/Linearization.lean)), the time-varying system `ż = −(1 + sin t) z` ([LinearizationTimeVarying](RoboLean/Examples/LinearizationTimeVarying.lean)) and, in [ConverseLyapunovGeneral](RoboLean/Examples/ConverseLyapunovGeneral.lean), the four-bound converse on that system and a piecewise continuous rate without a state-transition matrix.

The manipulator of §5 is a first-order system in [ManipulatorSystem](RoboLean/Control/ManipulatorSystem.lean), with the error dynamics of computed torque in [ErrorDynamics](RoboLean/Control/ErrorDynamics.lean) and shared matrix lemmas in [MatrixAnalysis](RoboLean/Control/MatrixAnalysis.lean). Its controllers are in [ComputedTorque](RoboLean/Control/ComputedTorque.lean) (Proposition 4.8), [PDControl](RoboLean/Control/PDControl.lean), [PDAsymptotic](RoboLean/Control/PDAsymptotic.lean) and [PDFlow](RoboLean/Control/PDFlow.lean) (Proposition 4.9, with compensation of the gravity term, and the flow of its closed loop under local Lipschitz continuity), [AugmentedPD](RoboLean/Control/AugmentedPD.lean) (Proposition 4.10) and [WorkspaceControl](RoboLean/Control/WorkspaceControl.lean) (workspace dynamics, Lemma 4.11 and workspace computed torque). For every robot `(M, C, N)` of Chapter 6 §5, [RobotSystem](RoboLean/Control/RobotSystem.lean) proves Proposition 4.8, [RobotPDControl](RoboLean/Control/RobotPDControl.lean) Proposition 4.9 and [RobotAugmentedPD](RoboLean/Control/RobotAugmentedPD.lean) Proposition 4.10 (semiglobal, and global when the inertia matrix is constant and the Coriolis matrix is a bounded skew matrix, the class that a uniform bound on the Coriolis matrix forces) with the tracking workspace PD law of Exercise 9; [WorkspacePDControl](RoboLean/Control/WorkspacePDControl.lean) gives the workspace PD law. The examples are a one-link arm ([OneLinkControl](RoboLean/Examples/OneLinkControl.lean)), the planar two-link arm under PD and computed torque ([PlanarTwoLinkControl](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkControl.lean)), in workspace coordinates ([WorkspaceControl](RoboLean/Examples/WorkspaceControl.lean)), in absolute link angles ([RobotPDControl](RoboLean/Examples/RobotPDControl.lean)) and as a robot under augmented PD ([RobotAugmentedPD](RoboLean/Examples/RobotAugmentedPD.lean)); [ThreeLinkGravityPD](RoboLean/Examples/ThreeLinkGravityPD.lean) shows that the bare PD law (4.51) does not hold the three-link arm at rest in gravity, and [AugmentedPDEnergy](RoboLean/Examples/AugmentedPDEnergy.lean) that no single `ε` makes the skewed energy of Proposition 4.10 a global Lyapunov function for the planar arm. [ExponentialArrival](RoboLean/Control/ExponentialArrival.lean) names the constants of Definition 4.3's bound together with the radius of initial errors it covers, derives the time after which a solution stays within a tolerance, and proves that PD control with compensation of the gravity term converges exponentially to every set point on bounded sets of initial errors, with constants independent of the set point (Proposition 4.10 at a constant set point); [PlanarTwoLinkRegulation](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkRegulation.lean) discharges it for the planar two-link arm.

### Constrained and hand dynamics

[ConstrainedLagrangian](RoboLean/Dynamics/ConstrainedLagrangian.lean) formalizes Pfaffian constraints, Lagrange multipliers and the Lagrange–d'Alembert equations of MLS Chapter 6, and [SubstitutedLagrangian](RoboLean/Dynamics/SubstitutedLagrangian.lean) eq. [RedundantDynamics](RoboLean/Dynamics/RedundantDynamics.lean) with [PseudoInverse](RoboLean/Dynamics/PseudoInverse.lean), [Tendons](RoboLean/Dynamics/Tendons.lean) and [HandControl](RoboLean/Dynamics/HandControl.lean) cover redundant manipulators and grasps, tendon actuation and the hand torques (6.36), with Examples 6.3–6.5 in [RedundancyAndTendons](RoboLean/Examples/RedundancyAndTendons.lean). The robot operations of §5 are [RobotControl](RoboLean/Control/RobotControl.lean), the `control` operation with PD and computed torque as controllers and the force loop of p. 302, and [RobotAttach](RoboLean/Control/RobotAttach.lean), the stack of a list of robots and the `attach` operation with Lemma 6.1 for it and joint-to-workspace coordinates as an `attach`; Example 6.7 is a one-dimensional toy in [RobotAttach](RoboLean/Examples/RobotAttach.lean). ### Nonholonomic systems

[VectorField](RoboLean/Nonholonomic/VectorField.lean) gives Lie derivatives and the Lie bracket of MLS Chapter 7 as Mathlib's `lieBracket`, with the book's sign, Example 7.1 (for linear fields the bracket is minus the matrix commutator, and on hat matrices minus the twist bracket) and Proposition 7.1; [Distribution](RoboLean/Nonholonomic/Distribution.lean) gives distributions spanned by vector fields, regular and involutive distributions and the generator criterion, and [Filtration](RoboLean/Nonholonomic/Filtration.lean) the filtration, growth vectors, the degree of nonholonomy and the Lie algebra generated by the input fields, with Exercises 10 and 11 in [FiltrationBrackets](RoboLean/Nonholonomic/FiltrationBrackets.lean) and two-input growth vectors in [TwoInputs](RoboLean/Nonholonomic/TwoInputs.lean). [Integrability](RoboLean/Nonholonomic/Integrability.lean) proves the necessity half of Frobenius' theorem and Proposition 7.4 ("only if") for Pfaffian constraints; the sufficiency half is a named statement, not proved. [Controllability](RoboLean/Nonholonomic/Controllability.lean) defines drift-free systems with piecewise constant inputs, reachable sets and local controllability, states Chow's theorem as a named statement, and proves the reversal and rescaling of trajectories (Exercise 4, with a corrected input). The rolling disk, the kinematic car, the spherical finger on a plane and the hopping robot are in [NonholonomicDisk](RoboLean/Examples/NonholonomicDisk.lean), [NonholonomicCar](RoboLean/Examples/NonholonomicCar.lean), [NonholonomicFinger](RoboLean/Examples/NonholonomicFinger.lean) and [NonholonomicHopper](RoboLean/Examples/NonholonomicHopper.lean), and their growth vectors, degrees of nonholonomy, rank conditions and nonholonomy in [NonholonomicGrowth](RoboLean/Examples/NonholonomicGrowth.lean). Flows of fields with a bounded continuous derivative, their group property, each `φ^X_t` as a diffeomorphism with inverse the flow of `−X`, and commuting flows with the square (7.8) closing exactly when the bracket vanishes are in [FlowsReverse](RoboLean/Nonholonomic/FlowsReverse.lean), [FlowsGroup](RoboLean/Nonholonomic/FlowsGroup.lean), [FlowsTwoSided](RoboLean/Nonholonomic/FlowsTwoSided.lean) (the flow for all real times) and [CommutingFlows](RoboLean/Nonholonomic/CommutingFlows.lean), with instances in [CommutingFlowsSine](RoboLean/Examples/CommutingFlowsSine.lean), [FlowsGroupSine](RoboLean/Examples/FlowsGroupSine.lean) and [FlowsTwoSidedSine](RoboLean/Examples/FlowsTwoSidedSine.lean). Four printed formulas are corrected; the [conventions](research/reports/geometry-conventions.md) and the [ledger](research/reports/mls-coverage-ledger.md) record them.

Chapter 8's exact core: [ChainedForm](RoboLean/Nonholonomic/ChainedForm.lean) gives the one-chain system with Lemma 8.1 and the rank condition of Proposition 8.2; [Steering](RoboLean/Nonholonomic/Steering.lean), with the period integrals of [SteeringIntegrals](RoboLean/Nonholonomic/SteeringIntegrals.lean), proves that the sinusoids of Algorithm 3 move `q_{k+2}` by `(a/4π)^k b/k!` and return the earlier coordinates, for every `k` and every initial state, [SteeringArbitrary](RoboLean/Nonholonomic/SteeringArbitrary.lean) proves Proposition 8.3 (Algorithm 3 steers the one-chain system to every configuration), and [SteeringSecondOrder](RoboLean/Nonholonomic/SteeringSecondOrder.lean) proves the two formulas of p. 363. [OptimalInputs](RoboLean/Nonholonomic/OptimalInputs.lean) proves Proposition 8.4 (`u̇ = Ω(q, p)u` with `Ω` skew, so the input has constant norm) from the necessary conditions (8.12)–(8.13), and Exercise 6's norm preservation. Brockett's system with the stationary inputs of (8.3), whose printed `q₃(1)` has the wrong sign, the `m`-input system (8.4)–(8.5), Engel's system and the chained form of the car (Example 8.3, as printed and corrected for (7.14)) are in [NonholonomicBrockett](RoboLean/Examples/NonholonomicBrockett.lean), [NonholonomicEngel](RoboLean/Examples/NonholonomicEngel.lean) and [NonholonomicCarChained](RoboLean/Examples/NonholonomicCarChained.lean); [SphereRolling](RoboLean/Nonholonomic/SphereRolling.lean) gives (8.34)–(8.36) for a sphere rolling on a plane, Theorem 8.6 for coordinate rectangles and the equation of Proposition 8.7, and [SphereRollingHand](RoboLean/Nonholonomic/SphereRollingHand.lean) the grasp constraint (8.30) as the control system (8.31).

### Lie groups and invariant forms

[InvariantMetrics](RoboLean/LieGroups/InvariantMetrics.lean) gives the frame-invariant metrics on `ℝⁿ` of MLS Appendix A (Lemma A.2 and Proposition A.3: the multiples `αI`, `α > 0`); [TwistForms](RoboLean/LieGroups/TwistForms.lean) shows that the Euclidean inner product of twists is not frame invariant and classifies the `Ad`-invariant symmetric forms on `se(3)` as `[[0, βI], [βI, γI]]` (Proposition A.5), so that no bi-invariant metric exists (Corollary A.5.1), and [LeftInvariantForms](RoboLean/LieGroups/LeftInvariantForms.lean) states these for quadratic forms on `SE(3)` extended by left translation, with Lemma A.4. [HyperbolicKilling](RoboLean/LieGroups/HyperbolicKilling.lean) gives the hyperbolic metric, which is the reciprocal product, and the Killing form as the trace of `ad`, with the magnitude and pitch of a twist through them (Examples A.21–A.23), and [KillingVolumeForm](RoboLean/LieGroups/KillingVolumeForm.lean) the volume form on `so(3)`. [ReciprocalScrewSystems](RoboLean/LieGroups/ReciprocalScrewSystems.lean) gives the reciprocal systems of Exercise 2.17, and [JacobianFrames](RoboLean/LieGroups/JacobianFrames.lean) the frame dependence of `σ_min` and the frame independence of `det J` (Examples A.26–A.27). At the level of matrix groups, [LeftInvariantFields](RoboLean/LieGroups/LeftInvariantFields.lean) gives Proposition A.1 for diffeomorphisms and the Lie algebras of `GL(n)`, `SO(3)` and `SE(3)` as brackets of left invariant fields, [MatrixExponential](RoboLean/LieGroups/MatrixExponential.lean) and [CanonicalCoordinates](RoboLean/LieGroups/CanonicalCoordinates.lean) one-parameter subgroups, `T_e exp = id` and canonical coordinates of the first kind on `GL(n)`, [ExponentialClosedForms](RoboLean/LieGroups/ExponentialClosedForms.lean) the closed forms of Examples A.12 and A.15, and [GroupActions](RoboLean/LieGroups/GroupActions.lean) the orbits, the conjugation action and `Ad_g = T_e I_g` of Examples A.16–A.18.

### Design certificates

The five Design modules mirror, in Lean, target supervisors for the planar two-link arm that are checked with Veil in a separate evaluation project beside this repository, which uses its own Lean 4.32.0 toolchain; the guard is the workspace certificate of the planar arm. They are the [target supervisor](RoboLean/Design/PlanarArmSupervisor.lean) and its extensions with a [command timeout](RoboLean/Design/PlanarArmTimeoutSupervisor.lean), a [retry limit and tick budget](RoboLean/Design/PlanarArmBudgetSupervisor.lean), a [round-robin scheduler](RoboLean/Design/PlanarArmSchedulerSupervisor.lean) and a [wrap-around scheduler](RoboLean/Design/PlanarArmWrapSchedulerSupervisor.lean). ### Demo

[Supervisor](RoboLean/Demo/Supervisor.lean) is an executable timeout supervisor for the planar two-link arm over rational targets, with core imports only and a line protocol in [Main](RoboLean/Demo/Main.lean); [SupervisorRefines](RoboLean/Demo/SupervisorRefines.lean) proves that its guard decides the workspace guard exactly and that every state it reaches is a reachable state of the design supervisor, so the design invariants and the reachability of commanded targets hold on it, and [PlanarArmFrames](RoboLean/Demo/PlanarArmFrames.lean) joins the kinematic and dynamic frames of the arm. [ArrivalTime](RoboLean/Demo/ArrivalTime.lean) bounds, for the ideal continuous arm held at each commanded set point, the end-effector error by an exponential in time and derives an arrival time and an age bound that certifies arrival, and [ArrivalConstants](RoboLean/Demo/ArrivalConstants.lean) computes explicit constants for the demo's parameters and the age bound they certify; the [demo report](research/reports/planar-arm-demo.md) compares that bound with the demo's own timeout. A Python loop under [demo](demo) simulates the arm under PD control and asks the Lean executable for every supervisory decision: after `lake build`, which also compiles the supervisor to the native executable `planar-arm-supervisor` (run it with `lake exe planar-arm-supervisor`), run `uv run python planar_arm_demo.py` in `demo/`; the loop drives the native executable through `lake exe planar-arm-supervisor` unless the environment variable `ROBO_LEAN_SUPERVISOR` names another command, such as the interpreter `lake env lean --run RoboLean/Demo/Main.lean`. `uv run python veil_differential_test.py` in `demo/` replays random and scripted action traces through both Lean commands and through Veil's executable semantics of the supervisor model (in a separate Veil project, since the two use different Lean versions) and compares the states after every action; it is a test, not a proof. The [demo report](research/reports/planar-arm-demo.md) states what is kernel-checked and what is not; the compiler, the protocol, the floating-point simulation and the harness are unverified.

## Scope and open items

The library uses noncomputable definitions over exact reals. Euler inverse endpoint cases remain open; the converse of Definition 2.1 is proved. In Chapter 3, the spherical-wrist workspace remark, the singular value decomposition exercises and the open-ended parts of Exercises 1–4, 13 and 20–22 are open. In Chapter 4, the converse of Theorem 4.5 with the fourth bound is proved for fields with a continuous derivative bounded on `[0, ∞) × ℝⁿ`, and under a hypothesis that the solution flow is differentiable in the initial state; the fourth bound for fields that are only Lipschitz is open; Theorem 4.6 for `A(t)` with jumps would need Carathéodory solutions, which are not adopted. In Chapter 5, rolling contact is formalized, including the grasp kinematics with rolling (5.40)–(5.41) and Proposition 5.12 for two chart patches; Theorem 5.9, Exercise 19, the ellipsoid with three different axes of Exercises 14–15 and the contact set of two whole bodies are open. Chapter 6 §§1–5 are covered, including the `attach` and `control` operations; (6.32)–(6.33) are not selected, since the book gives no derivation of (6.33). Chapter 7 is covered in part: brackets, distributions, filtrations and growth vectors, the necessity halves of Frobenius' theorem and Proposition 7.4, reachable sets with the reversal of trajectories, and four examples with their nonholonomy; flows with their group property and commuting flows for vanishing brackets are proved, while the sufficiency half of Frobenius' theorem and Chow's theorem are named statements, not proved, since they need flows that depend smoothly (to every order) on their initial state and local frames, and Proposition 7.3, the Philip Hall basis and Exercise 11 from equal ranks are open. Chapter 8 is covered in part: the model systems, Lemma 8.1, Propositions 8.2 and 8.3, the displacement formulas of Algorithm 3 and of p. 363, Proposition 8.4 under the necessary conditions, Example 8.3, (8.30)–(8.31), (8.34)–(8.36), Theorem 8.6 for coordinate rectangles and the equation of Proposition 8.7; Theorem 8.6 for general regions, the geometry of Proposition 8.7, Exercise 6's `SO(m)` form and the book's approximate and informal steering methods are open or not selected. Appendix A is covered at the level of matrix groups and their Lie algebras; its manifold statements, Propositions A.6–A.7 and Theorems A.8–A.9 are open. The [ledger](research/reports/mls-coverage-ledger.md) and the [roadmap](research/reports/lean-library-roadmap.md) list the details. The selected exponential coordinate is not claimed smooth or unique. Frame/unit types and distinct point/vector types are not enforced; numerical refinement, physical-model fidelity, and user acceptance remain separate.

## Building

Install [elan](https://lean-lang.org/install/), then run from this directory:

```sh
lake exe cache get
lake build
```

`lake build` builds the library and the demo's native executable `planar-arm-supervisor` under `.lake/build/bin/`. Do not run `lake update`: the Mathlib revision is pinned in `lakefile.toml` and the resolved dependencies are checked in as `lake-manifest.json`. A pin change is a recorded decision, not a routine step.

## Development

Open the folder in VS Code with the Lean 4 extension for interactive checking. Add future project modules under `RoboLean/` and import them from `RoboLean.lean` when they should be part of the default build. The Lean version is in `lean-toolchain`; Mathlib is pinned in `lakefile.toml` and resolved in the checked-in `lake-manifest.json`.
