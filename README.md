# Robo Lean

Robo Lean is a Lean 4 library with pinned Mathlib, starting from the mathematical core of Murray, Li, and Sastry's *A Mathematical Introduction to Robotic Manipulation*. The [roadmap](research/reports/lean-library-roadmap.md), [coverage ledger](research/reports/mls-coverage-ledger.md), and [geometry conventions](research/reports/geometry-conventions.md) define the scope and assumptions.

## Library contents

### Rigid transforms and the rotation exponential

The library provides proper rotations and rigid-transform group/action laws in [RigidTransform](RoboLean/Geometry/RigidTransform.lean), a [homogeneous-matrix equivalence](RoboLean/Geometry/Homogeneous.lean), [cross-product preservation](RoboLean/Geometry/CrossProduct.lean), and [hat/vee maps](RoboLean/Geometry/Hat.lean). [HatPowers](RoboLean/Geometry/HatPowers.lean) proves the Euclidean square/cube identities, [RotationExp](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationExp.lean) packages the actual matrix exponential in `SO(3)` and proves its fixed-axis trajectory equation, and [Rodrigues](RoboLean/Geometry/Rodrigues.lean) gives unit, nonzero, and zero-axis formulas. [Rigid-transform examples](RoboLean/Examples/RigidTransform.lean) and [exponential examples](RoboLean/Examples/RotationExp.lean) state exact convention checks. [Smoke](RoboLean/Smoke.lean) remains the algebra setup check.

### Rotation representations

[RotationSurjectivity](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationSurjectivity.lean) proves unit-axis exponential representations for every proper rotation, including identity and half-turns, from a fixed unit axis of every proper rotation ([RotationAxis](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationAxis.lean)) and an orthonormal basis adapted to it ([RotationPlane](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationPlane.lean)). [RotationLog](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationLog.lean), [RotationParameters](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationParameters.lean), and [RotationAxisUniqueness](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationAxisUniqueness.lean) state principal-angle formulas, periodicity, and the exact uniqueness limits; [rotation-recovery examples](RoboLean/Examples/RotationLog.lean) check the identity and half-turn endpoints. [ElementaryRotations](RoboLean/Geometry/ElementaryRotations.lean) identifies the displayed axis-rotation matrices with actual exponentials; [EulerAngles](RoboLean/Geometry/EulerAngles.lean) proves forward Z-Y-Z formulas and a generic inverse requiring a positive radial term. [UnitQuaternion](RoboLean/Geometry/UnitQuaternion.lean) covers quaternion multiplication and the norm-one group.

### Twists and exponential coordinates

The twist foundation identifies ordered `(v, ω)` coordinates with an independent `se(3)` matrix carrier in [Twist](RoboLean/Geometry/Twist.lean). [TwistExp](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistExp.lean) uses the actual matrix exponential; [TwistExpClosedForm](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistExpClosedForm.lean) covers unit, nonunit, and zero-angular cases, and [TwistExpJacobian](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistExpJacobian.lean) proves the unit-axis translation coefficient invertible for `0 < θ < 2π`. [TwistSurjectivity](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistSurjectivity.lean) proves every rigid transform is attained, while [TwistLog](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistLog.lean) selects a noncomputable principal exponential coordinate that reconstructs the transform. [TwistTrajectory](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistTrajectory.lean) proves the differential equation and initial value of constant-twist trajectories (MLS (2.25)–(2.26), (2.37), (2.45)), and [twist examples](RoboLean/Examples/Twist.lean) check the coordinate order and the adjoint's linear part on exact inputs.

### Adjoints, brackets, and velocities

[TwistAdjoint](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistAdjoint.lean) and [TwistExpAdjoint](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistExpAdjoint.lean) connect coordinate adjoints, matrix conjugation, and actual exponentials. [TwistBracket](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistBracket.lean) and [AdjointBracket](RoboLean/Geometry/AdjointBracket.lean) connect the coordinate Lie bracket to matrix commutators and adjoint transformation. [RotationVelocity](RoboLean/Geometry/RotationVelocity.lean) (spatial and body angular velocities, Lemma 2.12), [RigidVelocity](RoboLean/Geometry/RigidVelocity.lean) (spatial and body velocities of pose curves, (2.53)–(2.61)) and [RigidVelocityComposition](RoboLean/Geometry/RigidVelocityComposition.lean) (Propositions 2.14–2.15 and Lemma 2.16) derive point, spatial/body, product, and inverse laws from actual derivative hypotheses; Example 2.5 is worked numerically in [Velocity](RoboLean/Examples/Velocity.lean) and symbolically in [PlanarVelocity](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarVelocity.lean).

### Screws

[TwistScrew](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistScrew.lean), [ScrewAxis](RoboLean/Geometry/ScrewAxis.lean), and [NormalizedTwist](RoboLean/Geometry/NormalizedTwist.lean) make finite pitch, affine axes, full-twist normalization, pure translation, and stationary cases explicit. [TwistScrewMotion](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistScrewMotion.lean) identifies independently defined geometric motion with the actual exponential and proves the fixed-line mechanism formulas. [Chasles](RoboLean/Geometry/Chasles.lean) gives geometric screw-displacement existence; [ScrewConjugation](RoboLean/Geometry/ScrewConjugation.lean) proves frame-change and signed-axis conventions for that motion. [TwistScrewInvariants](RoboLean/Geometry/TwistScrewInvariants.lean) shows how pitch, magnitude and finite axes behave under frame changes and signed scaling, and [screw examples](RoboLean/Examples/Screw.lean) check the parameters on exact inputs.

### Wrenches and reciprocity

[Wrench](RoboLean/Geometry/Wrench.lean), [WrenchScrew](RoboLean/Geometry/WrenchScrew.lean), and [Reciprocal](RoboLean/Geometry/Reciprocal.lean) cover force/torque transformations, power duality, wrench-screw parameters, and reciprocal coordinate spaces; [WrenchFrameInvariants](RoboLean/Geometry/WrenchFrameInvariants.lean) carries the wrench screw coordinates through a frame change. [WrenchWork](RoboLean/Geometry/WrenchWork.lean) proves actual interval-work identities with derivative and integrability hypotheses. [WrenchReciprocityGeometry](RoboLean/Geometry/WrenchReciprocityGeometry.lean) adds geometric screw models and a common-normal formula with explicit domain and angle assumptions. Exact examples include parameterized planar mechanisms; [wrench transfer](RoboLean/Examples/Wrench.lean), [wrench screw](RoboLean/Examples/WrenchScrew.lean) and [reciprocal](RoboLean/Examples/Reciprocal.lean) examples check the conventions against independently computed values.

### Manipulator kinematics

[SerialChain](RoboLean/Kinematics/SerialChain.lean) defines serial chains and product-of-exponentials forward kinematics (MLS Chapter 3); [Jacobian](RoboLean/Kinematics/Jacobian.lean) derives the spatial and body Jacobians from actual derivatives and [ForcePullback](RoboLean/Kinematics/ForcePullback.lean) the joint torques of an end-effector wrench. [Workspace](RoboLean/Kinematics/Workspace.lean), [PadenKahan](RoboLean/Kinematics/PadenKahan.lean) (Subproblems 1–3), [Singularity](RoboLean/Kinematics/Singularity.lean), [ClosedChain](RoboLean/Kinematics/ClosedChain.lean) and [Manipulability](RoboLean/Kinematics/Manipulability.lean) cover workspaces with explicit quantifiers, inverse-kinematics subproblems with their branch conditions, singular configurations, the closed-chain structure equation and manipulability measures; [MapJacobian](RoboLean/Kinematics/MapJacobian.lean) gives the Jacobian of a coordinate map and its rate, used by workspace control. The planar two-link arm is worked out in [PlanarTwoLink](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLink.lean) (forward kinematics), [PlanarTwoLinkWorkspace](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkWorkspace.lean) (the reachable annulus and a design certificate), [PlanarTwoLinkSingularity](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkSingularity.lean) and [PlanarTwoLinkInverseKinematics](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkInverseKinematics.lean) (eq. (3.16)), and the elbow manipulator of Examples 3.2 and 3.5 in [ElbowManipulator](RoboLean/Examples/ElbowManipulator.lean). [ExpSmooth](RoboLean/Geometry/ExpSmooth.lean) and [SerialChainSmooth](RoboLean/Kinematics/SerialChainSmooth.lean) prove that the matrix exponential along a line, the rotation and twist exponentials and the product-of-exponentials forward kinematics of every serial chain are smooth of every order, analytic included, and [RollingHandSmooth](RoboLean/Grasp/RollingHandSmooth.lean) carries this to the grasp kinematics (5.41), where the contact coordinates read from a finger become a `C¹` function of its joint angles at a contact.

### Open-chain dynamics

[RigidBodyInertia](RoboLean/Dynamics/RigidBodyInertia.lean), [InertiaTensor](RoboLean/Dynamics/InertiaTensor.lean), [MassMatrix](RoboLean/Dynamics/MassMatrix.lean) and [MassMatrixDerivative](RoboLean/Dynamics/MassMatrixDerivative.lean) build the manipulator inertia matrix of MLS Chapter 4 from link inertias and link Jacobians, with its differentiability and frame freedom. [Christoffel](RoboLean/Dynamics/Christoffel.lean) proves the skew symmetry of `Ṁ − 2C` for the Christoffel Coriolis matrix, [Lagrangian](RoboLean/Dynamics/Lagrangian.lean) gives Lagrange's equations and the equations of motion, [NewtonEuler](RoboLean/Dynamics/NewtonEuler.lean) the rigid-body Newton–Euler equations and [PotentialEnergy](RoboLean/Dynamics/PotentialEnergy.lean) the gravity term. For the planar two-link arm of Figure 4.4 ([PlanarTwoLinkDynamics](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkDynamics.lean), [PlanarTwoLinkLagrangian](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkLagrangian.lean)), the three-link arm of Example 4.3 ([ThreeLinkDynamics](RoboLean/Examples/ThreeLinkDynamics.lean), [ThreeLinkGravity](RoboLean/Examples/ThreeLinkGravity.lean)) and the SCARA of Example 4.4 ([ScaraDynamics](RoboLean/Examples/ScaraDynamics.lean)), the matrices computed from the general construction are compared with the printed ones; [SphericalPendulum](RoboLean/Examples/SphericalPendulum.lean) derives Lagrange's equations of Example 4.1.

### Stability and control

[Stability](RoboLean/Control/Stability.lean) and [StabilityTheorems](RoboLean/Control/StabilityTheorems.lean) state the Lyapunov notions of MLS Chapter 4 §4 and Theorems 4.4 and 4.5 (sufficiency), with the comparison bounds of quadratic forms in [QuadraticBounds](RoboLean/Control/QuadraticBounds.lean); [SolutionFlow](RoboLean/Control/SolutionFlow.lean), [LyapunovFlow](RoboLean/Control/LyapunovFlow.lean) and [GlobalFlow](RoboLean/Control/GlobalFlow.lean) supply solution flows, [LaSalle](RoboLean/Control/LaSalle.lean) proves Lasalle's principle (Theorem 4.7), and [LaSalleLocal](RoboLean/Control/LaSalleLocal.lean) its local form on a ball. [ConverseLyapunov](RoboLean/Control/ConverseLyapunov.lean) proves the converse of Theorem 4.5 with three bounds for autonomous Lipschitz fields, and [ConverseLyapunovGeneral](RoboLean/Control/ConverseLyapunovGeneral.lean) the converse with all four bounds for time-varying fields, under the hypothesis of [FlowDerivative](RoboLean/Control/FlowDerivative.lean) that the solution flow is differentiable in the initial state, which is discharged for linear time-varying systems. [Linearization](RoboLean/Control/Linearization.lean) and [LinearizationTimeVarying](RoboLean/Control/LinearizationTimeVarying.lean), with [StateTransition](RoboLean/Control/StateTransition.lean), [LinearTimeVarying](RoboLean/Control/LinearTimeVarying.lean) and the eigenvalue decay bound of [MatrixExponentialSpectrum](RoboLean/Control/MatrixExponentialSpectrum.lean), prove Theorem 4.6 for constant and time-varying linearizations; [StateTransitionPiecewise](RoboLean/Control/StateTransitionPiecewise.lean) shows that, among piecewise continuous `A(t)`, the library's state-transition matrices exist only for continuous `A`. The examples are the damped harmonic oscillator of Example 4.5 ([HarmonicOscillator](RoboLean/Examples/HarmonicOscillator.lean)), the nonlinear spring of Examples 4.6–4.7 ([NonlinearSpring](RoboLean/Examples/NonlinearSpring.lean)), the damped pendulum by linearization ([Linearization](RoboLean/Examples/Linearization.lean)), the time-varying system `ż = −(1 + sin t) z` ([LinearizationTimeVarying](RoboLean/Examples/LinearizationTimeVarying.lean)) and, in [ConverseLyapunovGeneral](RoboLean/Examples/ConverseLyapunovGeneral.lean), the four-bound converse on that system and a piecewise continuous rate without a state-transition matrix.

The manipulator of §5 is a first-order system in [ManipulatorSystem](RoboLean/Control/ManipulatorSystem.lean), with the error dynamics of computed torque in [ErrorDynamics](RoboLean/Control/ErrorDynamics.lean) and shared matrix lemmas in [MatrixAnalysis](RoboLean/Control/MatrixAnalysis.lean). Its controllers are in [ComputedTorque](RoboLean/Control/ComputedTorque.lean) (Proposition 4.8), [PDControl](RoboLean/Control/PDControl.lean), [PDAsymptotic](RoboLean/Control/PDAsymptotic.lean) and [PDFlow](RoboLean/Control/PDFlow.lean) (Proposition 4.9, with compensation of the gravity term, and the flow of its closed loop under local Lipschitz continuity), [AugmentedPD](RoboLean/Control/AugmentedPD.lean) (Proposition 4.10) and [WorkspaceControl](RoboLean/Control/WorkspaceControl.lean) (workspace dynamics, Lemma 4.11 and workspace computed torque). For every robot `(M, C, N)` of Chapter 6 §5, [RobotSystem](RoboLean/Control/RobotSystem.lean) proves Proposition 4.8, [RobotPDControl](RoboLean/Control/RobotPDControl.lean) Proposition 4.9 and [RobotAugmentedPD](RoboLean/Control/RobotAugmentedPD.lean) Proposition 4.10 (semiglobal, and global for a uniformly bounded Coriolis matrix) with the tracking workspace PD law of Exercise 9; [WorkspacePDControl](RoboLean/Control/WorkspacePDControl.lean) gives the workspace PD law. The examples are a one-link arm ([OneLinkControl](RoboLean/Examples/OneLinkControl.lean)), the planar two-link arm under PD and computed torque ([PlanarTwoLinkControl](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarTwoLinkControl.lean)), in workspace coordinates ([WorkspaceControl](RoboLean/Examples/WorkspaceControl.lean)), in absolute link angles ([RobotPDControl](RoboLean/Examples/RobotPDControl.lean)) and as a robot under augmented PD ([RobotAugmentedPD](RoboLean/Examples/RobotAugmentedPD.lean)); [ThreeLinkGravityPD](RoboLean/Examples/ThreeLinkGravityPD.lean) shows that the bare PD law (4.51) does not hold the three-link arm at rest in gravity, and [AugmentedPDEnergy](RoboLean/Examples/AugmentedPDEnergy.lean) that no single `ε` makes the skewed energy of Proposition 4.10 a global Lyapunov function for the planar arm.

### Grasping

[Contact](RoboLean/Grasp/Contact.lean), [FrictionCone](RoboLean/Grasp/FrictionCone.lean), [GraspMap](RoboLean/Grasp/GraspMap.lean), [ForceClosure](RoboLean/Grasp/ForceClosure.lean) and [SpatialGrasp](RoboLean/Grasp/SpatialGrasp.lean) cover the contact models, friction cones, grasp maps, force-closure (Propositions 5.1–5.3) and internal forces of MLS Chapter 5, with planar contact frames and grasp maps in [PlanarWrench](RoboLean/Grasp/PlanarWrench.lean). [HandJacobian](RoboLean/Grasp/HandJacobian.lean) and [Manipulability](RoboLean/Grasp/Manipulability.lean) give the hand Jacobian, the grasping constraint and the relations of Table 5.4; [Frictionless](RoboLean/Grasp/Frictionless.lean), [PositiveSpanBounds](RoboLean/Grasp/PositiveSpanBounds.lean), [Steinitz](RoboLean/Grasp/Steinitz.lean), [ExceptionalSurface](RoboLean/Grasp/ExceptionalSurface.lean), [AntipodalGrasp](RoboLean/Grasp/AntipodalGrasp.lean) and [SpatialAntipodal](RoboLean/Grasp/SpatialAntipodal.lean) cover grasp planning (Theorems 5.4–5.6 and the "if" direction of Theorem 5.7). Example 5.1 is in GraspMap, Example 5.2 in [BoxGrasp](RoboLean/Examples/BoxGrasp.lean), Examples 5.3–5.5 in [PlanarGrasp](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarGrasp.lean), hands grasping the box of Example 5.2 in [HandGrasp](RoboLean/Examples/HandGrasp.lean), the unnumbered SCARA example of §5.3 in [ScaraHand](RoboLean/Examples/ScaraHand.lean), and the examples and counterexamples of grasp planning in [GraspPlanning](RoboLean/Examples/GraspPlanning.lean).

### Rolling contact

[SurfaceChart](RoboLean/Grasp/SurfaceChart.lean) gives surface charts, the first fundamental form and the normalized Gauss frame of MLS Chapter 5 §6, [GeometricParameters](RoboLean/Grasp/GeometricParameters.lean) the metric, curvature and torsion (5.20)–(5.24), and [ContactFrameVelocity](RoboLean/Grasp/ContactFrameVelocity.lean) Lemma 5.10. [ContactCoordinates](RoboLean/Grasp/ContactCoordinates.lean) defines the contact coordinates and the relative pose of two surfaces in contact; [ContactEquations](RoboLean/Grasp/ContactEquations.lean) proves the algebraic core of Theorem 5.11 ((5.37) ⇔ (5.28)), Corollary 5.11.1 and the derivative-level core of Proposition 5.12, and [RollingContact](RoboLean/Grasp/RollingContact.lean) Theorem 5.11 and Corollary 5.11.1 along motions. [PlanarContactKinematics](RoboLean/Grasp/PlanarContactKinematics.lean) is Exercise 17(a). The charts of Examples 5.6 and 5.7 are in [SphereChart](RoboLean/Examples/SphereChart.lean), Examples 5.6–5.7 and Exercises 13 and 16 in [RollingContact](RoboLean/Examples/RollingContact.lean), and instances of Exercise 17(a) in [PlanarContact](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarContact.lean). The contact rotation and the sign of Example 5.6's curvature differ from the printed ones; the [conventions](research/reports/geometry-conventions.md) and the [ledger](research/reports/mls-coverage-ledger.md) record why.

[ContactBijection](RoboLean/Grasp/ContactBijection.lean) proves Proposition 5.12, the contact coordinates as a smooth function of the relative pose near a contact, by the inverse function theorem, and [RollingHand](RoboLean/Grasp/RollingHand.lean) gives hands with rolling contacts and the grasp kinematics (5.40)–(5.41), with the contact constraint derived for contact frames frozen at the contact; [PlanarRegularContact](RoboLean/Grasp/PlanarRegularContact.lean) is Exercise 17 for planar charts of any speed. Exercises 14 and 15 are in [SurfaceOfRevolution](RoboLean/Examples/SurfaceOfRevolution.lean), Exercise 17(b) in [PlanarEllipse](RoboLean/Examples/PlanarEllipse.lean), and a hand with two rolling fingertips on the box of Example 5.2 in [RollingHand](RoboLean/Examples/RollingHand.lean).

### Constrained and hand dynamics

[ConstrainedLagrangian](RoboLean/Dynamics/ConstrainedLagrangian.lean) formalizes Pfaffian constraints, Lagrange multipliers and the Lagrange–d'Alembert equations of MLS Chapter 6, and [SubstitutedLagrangian](RoboLean/Dynamics/SubstitutedLagrangian.lean) eq. (6.15); Examples 6.1–6.2 are in [ConstrainedDynamics](RoboLean/Examples/ConstrainedDynamics.lean). [CompositeDynamics](RoboLean/Dynamics/CompositeDynamics.lean), [StackedFingers](RoboLean/Dynamics/StackedFingers.lean), [ObjectChart](RoboLean/Dynamics/ObjectChart.lean) and [GraspChart](RoboLean/Dynamics/GraspChart.lean) give the hand–object system (6.16)–(6.26) with Lemma 6.1 and the link from the grasping constraint (5.15) to (6.18), with instances in [HandDynamics](RoboLean/Examples/HandDynamics.lean) and [HandChart](RoboLean/Examples/HandChart.lean). [PoseChart](RoboLean/Dynamics/PoseChart.lean) is a chart covering `SE(3)` by position and ZYZ Euler angles, with its body-velocity Jacobian, its singular set and the coverage statement, (6.18) and Exercise 4(b) in these coordinates, and (6.23); [its examples](RoboLean/Examples/PoseChart.lean) give a regular point and a singular one. [RedundantDynamics](RoboLean/Dynamics/RedundantDynamics.lean) with [PseudoInverse](RoboLean/Dynamics/PseudoInverse.lean), [Tendons](RoboLean/Dynamics/Tendons.lean) and [HandControl](RoboLean/Dynamics/HandControl.lean) cover redundant manipulators and grasps, tendon actuation and the hand torques (6.36), with Examples 6.3–6.5 in [RedundancyAndTendons](RoboLean/Examples/RedundancyAndTendons.lean). The robot operations of §5 are [RobotControl](RoboLean/Control/RobotControl.lean), the `control` operation with PD and computed torque as controllers and the force loop of p. 302, and [RobotAttach](RoboLean/Control/RobotAttach.lean), the stack of a list of robots and the `attach` operation with Lemma 6.1 for it and joint-to-workspace coordinates as an `attach`; Example 6.7 is a one-dimensional toy in [RobotAttach](RoboLean/Examples/RobotAttach.lean).

### Design certificates

The five Design modules mirror, in Lean, target supervisors for the planar two-link arm that are checked with Veil in a separate evaluation project beside this repository, which uses its own Lean 4.32.0 toolchain; the guard is the workspace certificate of the planar arm. They are the [target supervisor](RoboLean/Design/PlanarArmSupervisor.lean) and its extensions with a [command timeout](RoboLean/Design/PlanarArmTimeoutSupervisor.lean), a [retry limit and tick budget](RoboLean/Design/PlanarArmBudgetSupervisor.lean), a [round-robin scheduler](RoboLean/Design/PlanarArmSchedulerSupervisor.lean) and a [wrap-around scheduler](RoboLean/Design/PlanarArmWrapSchedulerSupervisor.lean). The link between the Veil models and these Lean statements is a recorded convention, not a kernel-checked import ([Veil report](research/reports/veil-robotics-verification.md)).

### Demo

[Supervisor](RoboLean/Demo/Supervisor.lean) is an executable timeout supervisor for the planar two-link arm over rational targets, with core imports only and a line protocol in [Main](RoboLean/Demo/Main.lean); [SupervisorRefines](RoboLean/Demo/SupervisorRefines.lean) proves that its guard decides the workspace guard exactly and that every state it reaches is a reachable state of the design supervisor, so the design invariants and the reachability of commanded targets hold on it, and [PlanarArmFrames](RoboLean/Demo/PlanarArmFrames.lean) joins the kinematic and dynamic frames of the arm. A Python loop under [demo](demo) simulates the arm under PD control and asks the Lean executable for every supervisory decision: after `lake build`, which also compiles the supervisor to the native executable `planar-arm-supervisor` (run it with `lake exe planar-arm-supervisor`), run `uv run python planar_arm_demo.py` in `demo/`; the loop drives the native executable through `lake exe planar-arm-supervisor` unless the environment variable `ROBO_LEAN_SUPERVISOR` names another command, such as the interpreter `lake env lean --run RoboLean/Demo/Main.lean`. `uv run python veil_differential_test.py` in `demo/` replays random and scripted action traces through both Lean commands and through Veil's executable semantics of the supervisor model (in a separate Veil project, since the two use different Lean versions) and compares the states after every action; it is a test, not a proof. The [demo report](research/reports/planar-arm-demo.md) states what is kernel-checked and what is not; the compiler, the protocol, the floating-point simulation and the harness are unverified.

## Scope and open items

The library uses noncomputable definitions over exact reals. Quaternion-to-rotation and half-angle/group-map results, Euler inverse endpoint cases, remaining reciprocal-geometry cases, and the converse characterization of rigid maps remain open. In Chapter 4, the converse of Theorem 4.5 with the fourth bound is proved under a hypothesis that the solution flow is differentiable in the initial state, discharged only for linear time-varying systems, so the fourth bound for merely Lipschitz fields is open; Theorem 4.6 for `A(t)` with jumps would need Carathéodory solutions, which are not adopted. In Chapter 5, rolling contact is formalized, including the grasp kinematics with rolling (5.40)–(5.41) and Proposition 5.12 for two chart patches; Theorem 5.9, Exercise 19, the ellipsoid with three different axes of Exercises 14–15 and the contact set of two whole bodies are open. Chapter 6 §§1–5 are covered, including the `attach` and `control` operations; (6.32)–(6.33) are not selected, since the book gives no derivation of (6.33). Chapters 7–8 are open. The [ledger](research/reports/mls-coverage-ledger.md) and the [roadmap](research/reports/lean-library-roadmap.md) list the details. The selected exponential coordinate is not claimed smooth or unique. Frame/unit types and distinct point/vector types are not enforced; numerical refinement, physical-model fidelity, and user acceptance remain separate.

## Building

Install [elan](https://lean-lang.org/install/), then run from this directory:

```sh
lake exe cache get
lake build
```

`lake build` builds the library and the demo's native executable `planar-arm-supervisor` under `.lake/build/bin/`. Do not run `lake update`: the Mathlib revision is pinned in `lakefile.toml` and the resolved dependencies are checked in as `lake-manifest.json`. A pin change is a recorded decision, not a routine step.

## Development

Open the folder in VS Code with the Lean 4 extension for interactive checking. Add future project modules under `RoboLean/` and import them from `RoboLean.lean` when they should be part of the default build. The Lean version is in `lean-toolchain`; Mathlib is pinned in `lakefile.toml` and resolved in the checked-in `lake-manifest.json`.
