# Research reports

Prepared 2026-10-08 for a Lean robotics library based on Murray, Li, and Sastry's *A Mathematical Introduction to Robotic Manipulation*.

## Reading order

1. [MLS-to-Lean assessment](mls-lean-assessment.md): source-backed coverage, potential payoffs, assumptions, and limits across geometry, dynamics, control, design, planning, and learning.
2. [Lean library roadmap](lean-library-roadmap.md): work packages, dependencies, evidence requirements, and the current implementation status.
3. [MLS coverage ledger](mls-coverage-ledger.md) and [geometry conventions](geometry-conventions.md): source-level obligations, formal status, and explicit coordinate choices.
4. [Veil and robotics verification](veil-robotics-verification.md): transition-system verification alongside mathematical formalization and Z3, including current trust and compatibility limits.
5. [Planar arm demo](planar-arm-demo.md): an executable supervisor with a kernel-checked refinement, arrival times for the ideal arm, a simulation loop, and its trust boundary.

## Intent and status

The goal is to translate most of the book's mathematical core into a reusable baseline for multiple robotics research directions. Workspace-defined design is one example. Earlier Z3 work in the R-DTLGN project establishes prior experience with SMT encodings; it does not limit this project's application scope.

As of commit `6342e10` (2026-10-28) the library has 215 modules, all imported by `RoboLean.lean`; the demo's executable entry point is a further, unimported file. They cover Chapter 2 (rotations, rigid transforms, exponentials and their coordinates, twists, screws, velocities, adjoints, brackets, wrenches and reciprocity, the quaternion rotation map with its double cover, and the common normal of two screw axes); Chapter 3 (product-of-exponentials kinematics and its smoothness of every order, Jacobians and their derivative form, force pullback, workspaces, Paden–Kahan subproblems with the eight solutions of the elbow manipulator, singularities, closed chains and parallel mechanisms with Gruebler's formula and their kinematic and actuator singularities, self-motions, manipulability); Chapter 4 (the inertia matrix, Christoffel symbols, Lagrange's and the Newton–Euler equations, Lyapunov stability with Theorems 4.4–4.7, including the converse of Theorem 4.5 with all four bounds under a hypothesis that the solution flow is differentiable in the initial state, computed torque, PD and augmented PD control with Propositions 4.8–4.10 for every robot `(M, C, N)`, exponential set-point regulation with named constants and arrival times, workspace dynamics and the workspace PD laws); Chapter 5 §§2–6 (contact models, grasp maps, force-closure, the hand Jacobian, manipulability, grasp planning, and rolling contact: surface charts, the Gauss frame, the geometric parameters, Lemma 5.10, the contact coordinates, Theorem 5.11, Corollary 5.11.1, Proposition 5.12 for two chart patches, the grasp kinematics with rolling (5.40)–(5.41) with hands with rolling contacts, Examples 5.6–5.7 and Exercises 13, 14, 15 (two equal axes), 16 and 17); Chapter 6 §§1–5 (Pfaffian constraints and multipliers, the hand–object system, a chart of `SE(3)` with (6.23), redundancy, tendons, hand control, and the `attach` and `control` operations on robots); Chapter 7 in part (Lie brackets with the book's sign, distributions, filtrations, growth vectors and the degree of nonholonomy, the necessity halves of Frobenius' theorem and Proposition 7.4, drift-free systems and reachable sets with the reversal of trajectories, and the disk, car, spherical-finger and hopper examples with their nonholonomy; Frobenius' sufficiency and Chow's theorem as named statements); design modules that mirror Veil supervisors for the planar two-link arm; and the [planar arm demo](planar-arm-demo.md), an executable supervisor with a kernel-checked refinement and arrival times for the ideal arm. The [repository README](../../README.md) links the modules; the [conventions](geometry-conventions.md) state the domains and remaining interpretation limits.

**Verified - formal checking:** for the library as of commit `6342e10`, `lake build` succeeds and a whole-library axiom audit of the 215 imported modules reports 7107 constants, each depending on at most `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`, with no other axiom, no `axiom` declaration, and no `opaque` stand-in. The [ledger](mls-coverage-ledger.md), with the rows V01–V22 selected by commit `6342e10`, counts 315 selected MLS obligations, 310 of them Verified in full; G40 is an assessment row, V10 and V14 are named statements, not proved, and V11 and V18 are partial. The twenty-eight design rows C01–C28 are counted separately; C25, the executable's line protocol, and C27, the differential test against the Veil model, are tested, not proved; C26 is a Veil-side check. Counts are not independent theorems or a whole-book percentage. Raw check outputs (build logs, axiom audits, instance files) are kept in the maintainers' development records outside the repository; the published evidence anchor is the commit named in each ledger row.

Definitions remain noncomputable over exact reals; frames, physical units, and separate point/vector types are not enforced. A global selected exponential coordinate is supplied, but smoothness and uniqueness are not. Euler inverse endpoint cases, the fourth bound of Theorem 4.5 for merely Lipschitz fields (the differentiable-flow hypothesis is discharged only for linear time-varying systems), Theorem 4.6 for `A(t)` with jumps (Carathéodory solutions are not adopted), Theorem 5.9, Exercise 19, the three-axis ellipsoid of Exercises 14–15 and the contact set of two whole bodies in Chapter 5, the sufficiency half of Frobenius' theorem, Chow's theorem, Proposition 7.3 and the Philip Hall basis in Chapter 7, Chapter 8, and exhaustive book coverage remain open. The work theorems do not establish energy balance, contact or actuator feasibility, calibration, numerical quadrature, or physical-model fidelity. User acceptance remains separate from proof checking.
