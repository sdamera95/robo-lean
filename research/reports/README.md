# Research reports

Prepared 2026-10-08 for a Lean robotics library based on Murray, Li, and Sastry's *A Mathematical Introduction to Robotic Manipulation*.

## Reading order

1. [MLS-to-Lean assessment](mls-lean-assessment.md): source-backed coverage, potential payoffs, assumptions, and limits across geometry, dynamics, control, design, planning, and learning.
2. [Lean library roadmap](lean-library-roadmap.md): work packages, dependencies, evidence requirements, and the current implementation status.
3. [MLS coverage ledger](mls-coverage-ledger.md) and [geometry conventions](geometry-conventions.md): source-level obligations, formal status, and explicit coordinate choices.
4. [Veil and robotics verification](veil-robotics-verification.md): transition-system verification alongside mathematical formalization and Z3, including current trust and compatibility limits.
5. [Planar arm demo](planar-arm-demo.md): an executable supervisor with a kernel-checked refinement, arrival times for the ideal arm, a simulation loop, and its trust boundary.

## Intent and status

The goal is to translate most of the book's mathematical core into a reusable baseline for multiple robotics research directions. Workspace-defined design is one example. Earlier Z3 work in the R-DTLGN project establishes prior experience with SMT encodings; it does not limit this project's application scope.

As of commit `8b52cdc` (2026-11-03) the library has 278 modules, all imported by `RoboLean.lean`; the demo's executable entry point is a further, unimported file. They cover Chapter 2 (rotations, rigid transforms, exponentials and their coordinates, twists, screws, velocities, adjoints, brackets, wrenches and reciprocity, the quaternion rotation map with its double cover, the common normal of two screw axes, the converse of Definition 2.1, the planar group `SE(2)`, the Cayley parameters and the hybrid velocity); The twenty-nine design rows C01–C29 are counted separately; C25, the executable's line protocol, and C27, the differential test against the Veil model, are tested, not proved; C26 is a Veil-side check; C29, the differentiable dependence of solutions on their initial conditions, is kernel-checked. Raw check outputs (build logs, axiom audits, instance files) are kept in the maintainers' development records outside the repository; the published evidence anchor is the commit named in each ledger row.

Definitions remain noncomputable over exact reals; frames, physical units, and separate point/vector types are not enforced. A global selected exponential coordinate is supplied, but smoothness and uniqueness are not. Euler inverse endpoint cases, the fourth bound of Theorem 4.5 for fields that are only Lipschitz (the differentiable-flow hypothesis is discharged for fields with a continuous bounded derivative), Theorem 4.6 for `A(t)` with jumps (Carathéodory solutions are not adopted), Theorem 5.9, Exercise 19, the three-axis ellipsoid of Exercises 14–15 and the contact set of two whole bodies in Chapter 5, the sufficiency half of Frobenius' theorem, Chow's theorem, Proposition 7.3 and the Philip Hall basis in Chapter 7, Theorem 8.6 for general regions and the approximate steering methods of Chapter 8, the manifold statements of Appendix A with Propositions A.6–A.7 and Theorems A.8–A.9, and exhaustive book coverage remain open. The work theorems do not establish energy balance, contact or actuator feasibility, calibration, numerical quadrature, or physical-model fidelity. User acceptance remains separate from proof checking.
